\section{Limitations and conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

\subsection{Limitations}

The corpus is small, and the consequence can be stated precisely rather than merely admitted. The test split holds 201 sentences, which gives a 95\% bootstrap confidence interval of roughly $\pm 0.06$ on any $F_1$ reported over it. Two models separated by less than about 0.12 cannot be distinguished on this data. That bound is why the fine-tuned model and the best zero-shot checkpoint are reported as indistinguishable rather than as tied, and it should be read as a property of the corpus rather than of the models.

Every sentence comes from Wikipedia article summaries. The register is encyclopaedic and entity-dense, and nothing here shows that the results carry over to news, speech or social media. The comparison with WikiANN-sq is a comparison between two samples of the same source, which makes it a clean test of annotation method and a weak test of domain
generality.

Half of the corpus carries the model's fingerprints. 399 of the 820 sentences (49\%) were annotated by someone who could see the model's suggestions, and the assistance experiment shows that such annotators converge on the model's output. Those labels are therefore not independent of the pre-annotation system, and a model evaluated on them is partly being scored against a standard that another model helped write. The from-scratch half is free of this, and any evaluation that needs to be independent of the pre-annotation model should be restricted to it. This is the reason the assistance condition was designed to be measured rather than assumed, and the measurement is unfavourable enough that the design choice to pre-annotate at all deserves reconsideration in future work.

Disagreements on the shared sentences were resolved by token-level majority vote, not by a person. Twelve of the twenty shared sentences were not unanimous. Majority vote is reproducible and it is documented, but it is not adjudication, and a systematic error that most annotators shared would survive it untouched.

One annotator of ten never used the head label, marking no narrower head on any of 73 multi-token entities where peers did so between 10\% and 49\% of the time. Their sentences score 0.696 against 0.804 for the rest of the test split, which is the direction their omission predicts. Because they contribute 18 of 201 test sentences the reported head-view figure is depressed by about 0.008, inside seed variance. The defect is real and was caught by an automated check on annotation rates rather than by agreement statistics, which cannot see it. It is also evidence that a two-boundary protocol costs compliance, which is a finding about the method and not only a flaw in one person's work.

Training is not reproducible bit for bit on the hardware used. Two independent sets of three seeds, run under identical settings, gave WikiANN cross-benchmark means of 0.548 and 0.551 with individual runs between 0.518 and 0.574. Seeding does not fix this on Apple silicon, and the seed standard deviations quoted throughout should be treated as an upper bound that also absorbs hardware noise.

The annotator group is small and self-selected. Ten volunteers, five per condition in the assistance experiment, is enough for the effect observed but not enough to say anything about how the effect varies with experience or with the quality of the model's suggestions. The exact permutation test used there reaches $p = 0.004$, which is the smallest value a five against five design can produce, so the design is at its detection ceiling rather than comfortably above it.

\subsection{Conclusion}

This report set out to build a hand-verified Albanian NER corpus and to measure the parts of the annotation process that are usually assumed. The corpus holds 820 sentences and 1\,207 entities annotated by ten people, with agreement reported rather than asserted.

Three results follow from it. Rank order on WikiANN-sq does not predict performance on full-length Albanian prose, and the individual score changes are large and significant even though the rank statistic over four models is not. Pre-annotating with a large language model measurably moved what annotators produced, pulling them toward the model's own output, and did not measurably save them time. The choice between annotating full phrases and annotating proper-name heads is worth about 0.09 $F_1$ on identical data, a difference that exists entirely in where span edges fall rather than in which entities are found.

The annotation effort also pays for itself in the ordinary sense. Four hundred and eighty-three hand-annotated sentences outperform five thousand automatically projected ones by 0.132 $F_1$ on real prose, and combining the two corpora matches the better specialist on each benchmark at no measurable cost.

None of these findings required a large corpus, which is the argument for having built a small one carefully. The obvious next steps are a second domain beyond Wikipedia, a second adjudicator on a subset to bound adjudication bias, and a larger annotator pool if the assistance effect is to be characterised rather than merely detected.
