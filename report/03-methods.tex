\subsection{Corpus construction}
\label{sec:method:corpus}

Candidate sentences were drawn from Albanian Wikipedia through the MediaWiki REST API's
random-article endpoint, which returns an article summary rather than a full page. From
900 randomly sampled articles, \texttt{stanza}~\cite{bib:stanza} produced 3\,113
sentences, of which 426 fell outside the 8--40 token range and were discarded.

Automatic extraction and tokenisation corrupt a measurable share of sentences, so the
remainder passed through a quality filter (Table~\ref{tab:filter}). Every rule was
calibrated against a held-out sample rather than chosen a~priori, and the calibration
changed the rules twice. A naive ``an isolated lowercase letter indicates a split word''
test flags the unit symbol in \emph{400~m} and \emph{1--2~m}, and the Spanish particle in
\emph{Diego Rodríguez de Silva y Velázquez} --- the latter a sentence containing a person
entity. The final rule therefore exempts letters adjacent to a number, letters between two
capitalised tokens, and uppercase letters, which in this corpus are Roman numerals and
initials.

A separate defect proved more consequential. Tokenisation occasionally leaves punctuation
welded to a word, so that \emph{(Theron)} arrives as a single token. This is not cosmetic:
the model and the human annotator both operate on the same token sequence, so neither can
express the span \emph{Theron} if the tokeniser never offered it. Across the pool, 11.7\%
of sentences contained such a token and 156 of those tokens were capitalised, i.e.\ names.
A repair step splits bracket and quote characters from token edges while deliberately
leaving apostrophes intact, since Albanian contractions (\emph{t'}, \emph{s'}) and borrowed
names (\emph{d'Azur}) legitimately carry them. The affected share fell to 0.3\%, all of
them the apostrophe cases the repair declines to touch.

Finally, exact duplicates were removed and near-identical stub sentences capped at three
per template family. Albanian Wikipedia contains generated geography stubs that differ only
in their figures; one such template occurred fifteen times. These are genuine negatives ---
a model must learn that a sentence may contain no entities --- but fifteen near-copies add
nothing over three while consuming annotation effort. The final pool holds 2\,500 sentences
from 865 distinct articles, median length 20 tokens.

\subsection{Pre-annotation with a large language model}
\label{sec:method:prelabel}

Each sentence is presented to Claude Sonnet~5 as a numbered token list, and the model
returns entity spans as inclusive token-index ranges under a constrained JSON schema. Spans
rather than a per-token tag sequence: a parallel tag array can silently disagree in length
with its input, whereas a span list either validates or does not. Every returned span is
checked for range, type and overlap before conversion to BIO, so a malformed response
cannot produce a malformed tag sequence; rejections are counted by reason.

The model also returns the surface text it believes it selected. Where that disagrees with
the tokens at the given indices, the indices win and the disagreement is recorded, giving a
direct measure of how often the model miscounts.

Pre-annotation is performed per batch rather than over the whole pool. The prompt is
versioned and its version forms part of the response-cache key, so revising the annotation
conventions invalidates cached labels by construction and cannot silently mix two
standards. Because only sentences about to be annotated are pre-labelled, a mid-campaign
revision costs nothing for work not yet issued.

\subsection{Annotation protocol}
\label{sec:method:annotation}

Annotation is carried out in a self-hosted Label Studio instance. Work is issued in
numbered batches of 100 sentences; an append-only ledger records every assignment, which
prevents a sentence being issued twice --- inflating apparent agreement --- or never
issued at all.

\paragraph{Two boundary conventions.}
Albanian's \emph{X i Y} construction is genuinely ambiguous about where a name ends.
\emph{Stacioni i Bramit} (``Bram station'') may reasonably be annotated as a single
location or as the proper name \emph{Bramit} alone, and published annotation standards
differ. Rather than fixing the choice in the data, annotators mark both: a \emph{full span}
covering the phrase and a \emph{head} covering the proper-name core. Two flat BIO views are
derived from the single annotation, so the convention remains a reporting decision. Most
entities are bare names for which the two coincide, so the additional effort is confined to
the ambiguous cases.

\paragraph{Agreement and the assistance condition.}
Each batch embeds the same 20 shared sentences, so every annotator contributes to
inter-annotator agreement without a separate assignment. Odd-numbered batches are issued
with the model's suggestions visible and even-numbered batches without them. Because both
contain the same shared sentences, this yields the same sentences annotated under both
conditions --- a between-subjects design, which avoids the memory effects that would arise
from having one annotator work both ways.

Agreement is reported three ways: Fleiss'~$\kappa$~\cite{bib:fleiss1971} across all
annotators, pairwise Cohen's~$\kappa$~\cite{bib:cohen1960}, and pairwise span-level $F_1$.
The $\kappa$ statistics are chance-corrected but flattered by the preponderance of
\texttt{O} tokens, on which annotators trivially agree; span-level $F_1$ scores one
annotator against another exactly as a model is scored, and is the harsher measure.
Sentences garbled by extraction are flagged rather than deleted, so the rate remains
reportable and the decision auditable.

\subsection{Models and training}
\label{sec:method:models}

Four publicly available checkpoints provide reference points, evaluated with word-level
token classification in which each word takes the label predicted for its first subword.
The same routine scores the fine-tuned model, so comparisons reflect models rather than
measurement procedures.

The fine-tuned system is \texttt{xlm-roberta-base}~\cite{bib:xlmr}, trained with AdamW, a
linear warmup schedule, and epoch selection on a held-out development split. Every
configuration is trained under several random seeds.

\subsection{Active learning}
\label{sec:method:al}

The active learning comparison is a simulation over the annotated pool: labels are hidden
and revealed only as a strategy requests them. This is standard practice and is the only
practical option here, since a live loop would require annotators on standby between rounds
and could not be replayed under a different seed.

Two strategies are compared. \emph{Random} samples uniformly. \emph{Uncertainty} ranks
candidates by mean per-token predictive entropy --- mean rather than sum, so that the
strategy does not simply prefer long sentences and thereby confound informativeness with
annotation cost. Each round retrains from scratch on the labelled subset.

\subsection{Statistical treatment}
\label{sec:method:stats}

Two distinct uncertainties are reported, because conflating them understates both.

Inference over a frozen checkpoint is deterministic, so re-running with a different seed
reproduces the predictions exactly and seed variance is identically zero. What is uncertain
is the test set: a different sample of sentences would give a different score. This is
quantified by a percentile bootstrap over test sentences. Model comparisons use a
\emph{paired} bootstrap on the same resampled indices, so the shared difficulty of a
resample cancels out of the difference; this is substantially more powerful than checking
whether two independent intervals overlap.

Training, by contrast, has genuine run-to-run variance, so results are additionally
reported as mean~$\pm$~standard deviation across seeds.

A practical caveat applies to reproducibility. On the Apple silicon hardware used here,
two runs with identical seeds and hyperparameters produced test $F_1$ scores of 0.9187 and
0.9279, because the GPU kernels are not deterministic. Seeding therefore does not make
training bit-reproducible on this platform, no single run should be reported as \emph{the}
result, and the seed-to-seed standard deviation should be read as an upper bound on model
variance since it also absorbs this hardware noise.
